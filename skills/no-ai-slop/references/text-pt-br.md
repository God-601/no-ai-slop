# Brazilian Portuguese text

Use this reference when the target text is Brazilian Portuguese. Preserve
Portuguese variety when the user writes in another variety or asks for one.

## Match the context

- Identify the setting before changing register: conversation, schoolwork,
  public post, workplace message, journalism, or technical documentation.
- Keep the requested level of formality. Natural PT-BR can be formal, plain,
  colloquial, regional, or technical; do not default to slang or bureaucratic
  language.
- Prefer idiomatic Brazilian phrasing when it improves clarity. Preserve
  established English terms, names, and technical vocabulary when the audience
  uses them.
- Treat gerund use, formal openings, rhetorical transitions, emphatic contrast,
  and repeated conclusions as context-dependent. Revise only when they make
  this specific text vague, stiff, repetitive, or unlike the requested voice.

## Preserve meaning

Keep names, numbers, dates, negation, quotes, conditions, exceptions, causal
links, and the degree of certainty. Preserve differences such as “talvez”,
“deve”, “pode”, and “vai”. A smoother sentence must not turn a possibility
into a promise or remove a condition.

Use only facts supplied by the user or verified with an appropriate source.
Never add an excuse, personal anecdote, opinion, or local detail to make a text
sound more authentic.

## Final check

Read the result as the intended reader would. Check whether it sounds plausible
for the setting, still means the same thing, and avoids forced slang or
literal translation. If the register is unclear and would materially change
the rewrite, ask one focused question; otherwise make a reasonable choice and
name it briefly.
